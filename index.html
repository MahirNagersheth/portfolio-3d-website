    Grad School · LinkedIn Series

    How I Got Into a Top University

    The master's application process is like producing a movie — you're both the director and the protagonist. A 5-part behind-the-scenes look at what I learned building mine.

    Published · 5-part series
    Part 1 of 5 🎬 The Movie Analogy

    Over the past few weeks, I've been getting a lot of messages asking: "How did you get into CMU? What's the secret? What worked for you?"

    So here it is. Not a cheat code. Not a shortcut. Just a perspective I wish I had when I began this journey.

    I consider the master's application process to be like producing a movie — where you are both the director and the protagonist. Each element of your profile plays a role in your final production:

    • 🎥 Part 1 – The Budget (Test Scores & GPA)
    • 🎭 Part 2 – The Supporting Cast (Internships, Work, Projects)
    • 🎞️ Part 3 – The Trailer (Your Resume)
    • 🧬 Part 4 – The Script (Your SOP)
    • 🏆 Part 5 – The Final Cut (Weightage + Tribute)

    This series walks through how I built my own application film — and I hope it helps you build yours.

    Part 2 of 5 📊 The Budget — Test Scores & GPA

    Every movie needs a production budget. Your test scores are your basic funding. They won't win Oscars — but they will get your foot in the door.

    • GPA — Aim for a weighted 3.3+/4
    • IELTS — Target 7.5+ (some U.S. on-campus jobs require 8+)
    • GRE — Optional at times, but valuable

    The GRE is your profile equalizer. Got a lower GPA? A high GRE can offset it. The hidden truth: a strong GRE also significantly boosts your chances of securing scholarships.

    Budgets don't make movies great — but without one, no film gets made.

    Part 3 of 5 🎭 The Supporting Cast — Your Experiences

    No great protagonist stands alone. These are the characters that strengthen your story, show your depth, and bring your SOP to life.

    My experience-based priority list (for most programs):

    1. Work Experience
    2. Internships
    3. Projects
    4. Patents
    5. Research Papers / Book Chapters
    6. Extracurriculars
    7. NGO / Volunteering Work

    Your film can be action, research, or drama — but make sure the cast is relevant and well-developed. Pick the experiences that highlight growth, not just prestige.

    Part 4 of 5 🎞️ The Trailer — Your Resume

    Think of your resume as your movie trailer. It doesn't explain everything — but it has to convince them to watch.

    • Keep it 1 page or 2 — never 1.5
    • Use a clean, ATS-friendly format (score >75%)
    • Each role should have 3 bullet points max
    • Use numbers — quantify your impact (e.g., "Improved performance by 30%")

    Your resume should highlight impact, not effort. Show results, not just responsibilities. A good trailer makes people want to see the full story.

    Part 5A of 5 🧬 The Script — Your SOP

    If your GPA is the skeleton, your projects and work experience are the muscles, and your resume is the skin — then your SOP is the nervous system. It connects it all. It moves your story. It brings your application to life.

    My biggest tip? Make it circular. Think of your SOP like drawing a circle in the air. Your hook is where your journey begins. Your story expands, deepens, and reveals who you are. And in the end? You gently connect it back to where you began. That's your narrative arc. That's your closure. That's how you stay memorable.

    A structure that worked for me:

    • 🪝 Hook line — Catch attention in the first 5 seconds
    • 📖 Your origin — What experience or moment shaped you?
    • 🧠 Academic & professional journey — Support your goals with context
    • 🏫 Why this university and program — Be specific, go deep
    • 🎯 Career goals — Both short-term and long-term (5+ years out)
    • 🔁 Conclusion — Close the circle, return to your hook, leave them with clarity

    The SOP is the most overlooked but most impactful part of the application. You're not just explaining why you're applying. You're showing them why they should bet on you.

    Part 5B of 5 🏁 The Final Cut — Gratitude & Tribute

    You're not just submitting an application — you're producing the most important film of your life so far. And like any great film, it takes a team. I've been helped by professors, friends, mentors, and even strangers during this journey.

    There's one person I want to quietly thank in this process — someone who helped shape my SOP more than they probably realized. Sanjana Palkhiwala offered guidance, clarity, and support during a time I really needed it.

    She tragically passed away in the Air India crash in Ahmedabad. This is my way of remembering her impact. I carry it — and her kindness — with me moving forward.

    If you've made it to the end of this series, thank you. If you're starting your own application journey — I'm rooting for you.

    SOP · UC Berkeley

    My Statement of Purpose — MIMS at Berkeley

    From a family name bestowed by a Mughal emperor to a graduate application: the full SOP I wrote connecting my entrepreneurial roots to a career at the intersection of data and technology.

    Published application · 2024

    My family's entrepreneurial legacy dates back to the 1600s during the reign of the Mughal Empire, where my ancestors served as jewelers and financiers to Emperor Jahangir. They were more than just businessmen; they stood for civic leadership, protecting Jain temples in Ahmedabad and securing the city during turbulent times. As a mark of their contributions, they were bestowed the noble title of "Nagarsheth," meaning "lord of the city." Inspired by their legacy, I seek to forge my own path at the intersection of technology, data-driven decision-making, and entrepreneurship.

    From an early age, I was drawn to entrepreneurship, but I quickly realized that technology would be the driving force behind modern business success. In the 7th grade, I established a food stall with my friends. When customers didn't arrive, I took the initiative to promote our stall around the park, turning our venture into a success. Subsequently, in the 9th grade, I participated in the Young Entrepreneurs Association (YEA) event, applying my previous strategies to another food stall. However, due to market saturation with similar stalls, my profits remained meager. This experience taught me the essence of innovation in business. Learning from this, in the 10th, I took a different approach by collaborating with Canvas Champs to sell canvases — a unique product — which resulted in significantly higher profits. These experiences solidified my passion for entrepreneurship and highlighted the role of technology in scaling business solutions.

    Motivated by this realization, I pursued a Bachelor of Technology in Computer Science at Pandit Deendayal Energy University, focusing on machine learning, artificial intelligence, data analysis, and data management. One of my most impactful projects involved using five CNN models to identify arthritis in X-ray images, aiding in early detection and efficient medical diagnoses. Another research project utilized six machine learning models to predict vehicle fuel efficiency — presented at ICONAT 2024 and published in IEEE Xplore. Additionally, I developed a helmet detection system using YOLOv7, which reduced safety violations by 40%. These experiences reinforced my belief in the transformative power of data and technology in shaping industries.

    During my tenure as a data analyst at Rapidops Inc., I witnessed firsthand how businesses leverage data for strategic growth. I streamlined data pipelines, reducing retrieval time by 25%, and optimized workflows to improve operational efficiency by 15%. Similarly, at Matrubharti Technologies, I led the digitization of critical workflows, enhancing document processing speed by 25%.

    Beyond technical skills, I actively pursued leadership roles to enhance my ability to manage teams and drive impactful projects. As a project manager at the TIDE Foundation, I led a team of 85 volunteers across 14 schools. By integrating specialized clubs alongside the existing curriculum, we increased student engagement by 30% for over 500 students and raised ₹45,650 through strategic fundraising. My leadership extended to my role as Event Management Head of the Computer Science Club, overseeing 15 tech-focused events that attracted over 2,000 students.

    To achieve my long-term goal of building a data-driven technology business, I recognize the need for a deeper understanding of information management and its strategic applications. The MIMS program at UC Berkeley aligns perfectly with my aspirations, offering a curriculum that integrates technical expertise with business and policy considerations. Courses such as "Applied Machine Learning," "Data Science and Analytics for Policymaking," and "Information Visualization and User Experience" will provide the technical foundation, while "Managing Risk and Security" and "Technology, Law, and Policy" will equip me with strategic insight.

    Conversations with MIMS alumni have further reinforced my decision. I am particularly excited about learning from Professor Niloufar Salehi, whose research on human-computer interaction aligns with my interests in user-centric design, Professor Michael Rivera's work in organizational behavior, Professor Aditya Parameswaran's contributions to scalable data science, and Professor Hany Farid's pioneering research in digital forensics.

    Moreover, UC Berkeley's entrepreneurial ecosystem — the Sutardja Center for Entrepreneurship & Technology, the Berkeley Haas Entrepreneurship Program, SkyDeck, and the CITRIS Foundry — presents the ideal environment to bring ideas to life. The dynamic, interdisciplinary culture makes it the right place to grow as a technology leader.

    In the short term, I aim to leverage my MIMS education to drive digital transformation and data-driven strategies in tech startups. In the long term, I aspire to roles such as Chief Technology Officer or Innovation Strategist, integrating technical expertise with business acumen to drive advancement. I am confident that the MIMS program, with its interdisciplinary approach and unparalleled opportunities, will equip me with the skills and network to drive impactful change — continuing my family's legacy of leadership and innovation in a modern, data-driven world.
